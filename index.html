What is Ketamine Infusion Therapy?

Ketamine infusion therapy is a medical treatment in which a low, carefully controlled dose of ketamine is given through an IV over about 40 minutes. Ketamine was first used in the 1960s as an anesthetic, including for injured soldiers in the Vietnam War, and is now used at sub-anesthetic doses to treat depression, anxiety, PTSD and chronic pain.

Ketamine is not just a modern treatment

It has a place in history

Ketamine was first synthesized in 1962 by American chemist Calvin Stevens. It was developed as a safer anesthetic alternative to phencyclidine (PCP), which had produced serious side effects in patients.

The compound was patented in Belgium in 1963. Human trials began in 1964, when researcher Dr. Edward Domino observed that its main side effect was a brief, dream-like state. His wife, Toni Domino, coined the term “dissociative anesthetic” to describe it, and formal documented use in people and animals followed in 1966.

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved ketamine in 1970, and it was soon used on Vietnam War battlefields for fast pain control and emergency procedures. In the decades that followed it became a standard anesthetic in emergency rooms, intensive care units and operating rooms. Research in the late 1990s and 2000s revealed rapid antidepressant effects, and in 2019 the FDA approved esketamine, a ketamine-derived nasal spray, for treatment-resistant depression.

How Does Ketamine Therapy Work? What Does It Treat?

Ketamine infusions have a growing range of clinical uses and have helped many people whose symptoms did not respond to other treatments. Below are some of the most common conditions we treat in Kansas City.

Ketamine for Chronic Pain

People living with persistent pain conditions such as complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) have shown meaningful improvement with ketamine infusions. Ketamine blocks NMDA receptors that amplify pain signals in the brain and spinal cord, which is why it can work when other pain medications have not.

Ketamine for Depression and Anxiety

Depression is linked to weakened communication between brain cells. Controlled doses of ketamine boost glutamate signaling and support healthier mood pathways, often easing symptoms within hours to days rather than the weeks typical of standard antidepressants.

Ketamine for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

PTSD often comes with intense anxiety and depressive symptoms. By helping the brain form new connections and restoring healthier signaling, IV ketamine has been shown to reduce the severity of PTSD symptoms for many patients.

Ketamine for Bipolar Disorder

The depressive phases of bipolar disorder can be difficult to treat. Patients have responded well to ketamine infusions, which may rapidly lift mood and reduce suicidal thoughts when given alongside ongoing psychiatric care.

Ketamine for Migraine Headaches

Migraine is notoriously hard to control. By acting on the NMDA receptor, ketamine targets the brain regions involved in pain processing and has provided relief for some patients who found little benefit from other therapies.

Ketamine for Nerve Pain and Inflammatory Pain

Inflammation is the body’s normal defense against infection, but when the immune system targets healthy tissue it can cause ongoing pain, as in rheumatoid arthritis. IV ketamine can help reduce nerve and inflammatory pain and the distress that comes with it.

How Does Ketamine Infusion Therapy Work?

Ketamine works differently from most psychiatric medications. Instead of slowly adjusting serotonin levels, it acts on the brain’s glutamate system. It blocks NMDA receptors, which triggers a surge of glutamate that activates AMPA receptors – pathways tied to mood regulation – while also dampening the signals that keep pain switched on.

This activity prompts synaptogenesis: the growth of new connections between brain cells in areas weakened by chronic stress, depression and pain. Research suggests these strengthened pathways can persist well after the infusion itself, which is why a short series of treatments often produces lasting benefit.

Your Ketamine Infusion Therapy

Whether you come to us for pain, a mood disorder or another condition, the basics of your treatment day are the same. Please avoid all food and drinks other than water for six hours before your appointment. Arriving calm and unhurried also helps you get the most from your infusion.

Your comfort matters to us. Our team will settle you into a private infusion suite, and you are welcome to bring a blanket, pillow or anything else that helps you relax.

Before the infusion starts, you will receive anti-nausea medication to reduce possible side effects. Our clinical team, including an anesthesiologist, monitors you throughout the session to make sure you are tolerating the treatment well.

How is a ketamine IV infusion given?

How is ketamine IV therapy given? A small IV catheter is placed in a vein, and a precisely measured dose of ketamine flows slowly into your bloodstream over roughly forty minutes. Afterward we monitor you for about another forty minutes while the dissociative effects fade.

Once monitoring is complete you can go home, but you must arrange for someone to drive you. Ketamine can leave you feeling foggy for several hours, so driving yourself is not safe.
