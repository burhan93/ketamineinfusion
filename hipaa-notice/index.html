Patient Privacy

HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices

This notice describes how medical information about you may be used and disclosed and how you can get access to this information. Please review it carefully. In short: Balanced Ketamine uses your protected health information (PHI) to treat you, get paid and run our clinic; we share it for other reasons only when the law allows or you authorize it in writing; and you have the right to see, copy and correct your records.

Who follows this notice?

This notice applies to Balanced Ketamine, a ketamine infusion clinic located at 6700 W 121st St, Ste 300 A1, Overland Park, Kansas 66209, and to all of our physicians, nurses, staff, volunteers and business associates who handle your protected health information (PHI). PHI is information that identifies you and relates to your past, present or future physical or mental health, the care you receive, or payment for that care.

We are required by the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA) and its Privacy, Security and Breach Notification Rules (45 CFR Parts 160 and 164) to:

  • Keep your PHI private and secure;
  • Give you this notice of our legal duties and privacy practices;
  • Follow the terms of the notice currently in effect; and
  • Notify you promptly if a breach occurs that may have compromised the privacy or security of your information.

How do we use and share your health information?

We most often use and share your PHI for three purposes – treatment, payment and health care operations – without needing your written authorization.

Treatment

We use your information to provide, coordinate and manage your care. For example, our physician reviews your medical history and current medications before an infusion, and we may share records with your primary care physician, psychiatrist, therapist or the provider who referred you.

Payment

We use and share information to bill and collect payment from you, your health plan or a third-party financing company you choose. For example, we may give your insurer information about a treatment to confirm coverage or obtain prior authorization.

Health care operations

We use information to run our clinic, improve the quality of care and train staff. For example, we may review records to evaluate the safety of our infusion protocols, or share information with accountants, IT vendors or legal advisers who sign a Business Associate Agreement promising to protect it.

Appointment reminders and treatment information

We may contact you by phone, text, email or mail to remind you of appointments or to share information about treatment options or health-related services that may interest you. You can ask us to use a different method or address at any time.

When can we share information without your authorization?

The law allows or requires us to share your PHI in the following situations, subject to specific conditions:

  • As required by law – when federal, state or local law requires it.
  • Public health and safety – to report disease, injury, adverse reactions to medications, or suspected abuse, neglect or domestic violence, and to prevent or reduce a serious threat to anyone’s health or safety.
  • Health oversight – to agencies for audits, investigations, inspections and licensure.
  • Lawsuits and legal actions – in response to a court or administrative order, or to a subpoena when legal requirements are met.
  • Law enforcement – for limited purposes, such as identifying a suspect or responding to a valid warrant.
  • Coroners, medical examiners and funeral directors – as needed to carry out their duties.
  • Organ and tissue donation – to organizations that handle donation and transplantation.
  • Research – when an institutional review board or privacy board has approved the use under HIPAA rules.
  • Specialized government functions – for military, national security and protective services, as authorized by law.
  • Workers’ compensation – for claims, as permitted by state law.
  • Family and friends involved in your care – unless you object, we may share information relevant to their involvement, such as with the driver who takes you home after an infusion. In an emergency, we use professional judgment.
  • Business associates – with vendors who perform services for us and are contractually bound to protect your information.

When do we need your written permission?

We will not use or share your PHI for the following purposes unless you give us written authorization:

  • Most uses and disclosures of psychotherapy notes;
  • Marketing purposes, including the use of patient testimonials, photos or videos;
  • Sale of your PHI (we do not sell PHI); and
  • Any other use or disclosure not described in this notice.

You may revoke an authorization at any time by writing to our Privacy Officer. Revocation does not affect information already shared while the authorization was in effect.

Some types of information, such as substance use disorder treatment records from federally assisted programs (42 CFR Part 2), HIV/AIDS test results and certain mental health information, may have extra protection under federal, Kansas or Missouri law. When those laws are stricter than HIPAA, we follow them.

What are your rights over your health information?

You have the right to see and get copies of your records, ask us to correct them, limit what we share, choose how we contact you, get a list of certain disclosures, and get a paper copy of this notice.

Your rightWhat it means and how to use it
Access and copiesAsk to see or get an electronic or paper copy of your medical and billing records. We respond within 30 days and may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee.
AmendmentAsk us in writing to correct information you believe is incorrect or incomplete. We may say “no” but will explain why in writing within 60 days.
Confidential communicationsAsk us to contact you in a specific way (for example, a mobile phone only) or at a different address. We will agree to all reasonable requests.
RestrictionsAsk us not to use or share certain information for treatment, payment or operations. We are not required to agree, except that if you pay for a service in full out of pocket, you may ask us not to share it with your health insurer, and we will agree unless the law requires it.
Accounting of disclosuresAsk for a list of the times we shared your information in the six years before your request, other than for treatment, payment, operations and certain other exceptions. One list per year is free.
Paper copy of this noticeRequest a paper copy at any time, even if you agreed to receive it electronically.
Personal representativeIf someone has medical power of attorney or is your legal guardian, that person can exercise your rights. We will confirm their authority first.
Breach notificationBe notified if a breach of your unsecured PHI occurs.

To exercise any of these rights, contact our Privacy Officer using the details at the bottom of this page.

Is the website contact form secure for health information?

No. The appointment request forms on this website are intended for contact details only. Please do not include diagnoses, medications or other health information in a web form or email. Submitting a request does not create a physician–patient relationship. Our team will call you to collect medical information through secure channels. For details about cookies and online tracking, see our Cookie Policy.

How do you file a privacy complaint?

If you believe your privacy rights have been violated, you can file a complaint with us or with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. We will not retaliate against you for filing a complaint.

  1. With Balanced Ketamine: contact our Privacy Officer by phone at (913) 871-9888 or in writing at 6700 W 121st St, Ste 300 A1, Overland Park, KS 66209.
  2. With the Office for Civil Rights: write to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, 200 Independence Avenue S.W., Washington, D.C. 20201, call 1-877-696-6775, or visit hhs.gov/ocr/complaints.

Can this notice change?

We can change the terms of this notice, and the changes will apply to all information we hold about you. The current notice is always available in our office and on this page, and the effective date appears at the top.
